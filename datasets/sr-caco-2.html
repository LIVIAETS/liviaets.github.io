SR-CACO-2 — Confocal Microscopy Image Super-Resolution

A large-scale dataset for single-image super-resolution (SISR) in confocal fluorescence microscopy, with real low-resolution images captured directly by the microscope.

Abstract

Confocal fluorescence microscopy is one of the most accessible and widely used imaging techniques for studying biological processes at the cellular and subcellular levels. Scanning confocal microscopy captures high-quality images from thick 3D samples, yet suffers from well-known limitations such as photobleaching and phototoxicity caused by intense light exposure — limiting its use, especially for living cells. Cellular damage can be reduced by changing imaging parameters to lower light exposure, often at the expense of image quality.

Machine/deep-learning methods for single-image super-resolution (SISR) can restore image quality by upscaling lower-resolution (LR) images to high-resolution (HR). These methods have been successful on photo-realistic images, partly due to abundant public datasets — but the lack of public data limits their success in scanning confocal microscopy. We introduce SR-CACO-2, a large scanning confocal microscopy dataset of low- and high-resolution image pairs marked for three fluorescent markers. It enables evaluation of SISR methods at three upscaling levels (×2, ×4, ×8). SR-CACO-2 contains the human epithelial cell line Caco-2 (ATCC HTB-37), composed of 2,200 unique images captured with four resolutions and three markers, translated into 9,937 patches. We provide benchmarking results for 16 state-of-the-art methods representative of the main SISR families; results show limited success in producing high-resolution textures, indicating that SR-CACO-2 is a challenging problem. The dataset, code and pretrained weights are publicly available.

Capture

Diversity

To ensure technical and biological diversity, image acquisition was automated: each set of 100 images was stitched (10 × 10) into a tile, so each of 22 tiles represents 100 unique fields of view — 2,200 unique images, each captured at four scales for three markers. Collecting one tile across four resolutions takes ~12–16 hours. The 22 wells came from 4 independent experiments using 5–6 wells per plate, ensuring standard biological diversity.

An object-based analysis of the 2,200 high-resolution fields of view reveals ~16,800 multi-cellular objects. Cells were cultivated in a 3D protein matrix forming tissue-like cyst structures, with size variations spanning two orders of magnitude and substantial variation in aspect ratio and circularity — covering single-layered, multi-layered and solid phenotypes.

Real vs. interpolated low-resolution

We compared real (microscope) LR against bicubic LR. Their absolute pixel-wise difference can fall in [0, 50] and [200, 255], concentrated over cell regions. Real LR images are noisier (single scan) while HR images are averaged over nine scans; bicubic LR tends to be very smooth and even preserves structure from HR. This contrast confirms that bicubic LR cannot substitute for real LR in training and evaluation — real LR images are essential to simulate realistic deployment conditions.

Downstream tasks: cell detection & segmentation

Conclusion

The few existing SISR microscopy datasets are mostly private, limiting research. SR-CACO-2 addresses this gap with three scales (×2, ×4, ×8) plus HR, three protein markers under different lighting, and ~9k real LR/HR patch pairs, following standard, reproducible capture and evaluation protocols. Benchmarking shows current SISR methods cannot accurately reproduce HR images, making the dataset challenging. Beyond image quality, we evaluated downstream biology tasks such as cell detection and segmentation, where several methods showed promising results. SR-CACO-2 can drive progress in SISR for microscopy — from fixed to live imaging, opening the door to instantaneous inter-cellular observation with reduced cell damage.
